# Slack connector: routing from hydra.toml

The Slack connector (`connectors/slack/`) is one connector of [routing](diagrams/flow-route.mmd). It is not the Slack gateway, the operator's own chat surface (`gateways/slack.ts`, `CHAT_PLATFORM=slack`); [Slack Setup](slack.md) sets the two side by side. Who sent a message, where, and through which Slack app decide which specialist hears it, all from `<state>/hydra.toml` ([Configuration](#configuration); every key in [`hydra.example.toml`](hydra.example.toml)): `[slack.apps.<id>]` for each Slack app and its tokens, `[[rules]]` for which specialist hears which place, `reachable_by` for the roles that may reach a specialist. Routing reads who sent a message, where, and which apps it tags, never the rest of what it says.

**Where a message goes.** A conversation is an app, a channel and a thread (or a DM's top level). It is bound to a specialist when its first message arrives: by the first `[[rules]]` entry that matches the place, the app and the sender (a rule names a place, an app, or both; a specialist can be pinned to apps; when two apps share a channel, one is the owning app of each message: see [Apps and routing](slack.md#apps-and-routing)), else, for the operator's DM, `main`. A rule is the only place a specialist is heard: a specialist has no `listens`: every place it hears is a rule. Everything later in it, owed messages included, follows the binding: a config change re-routes only new conversations, a rename through `renamed_from` keeps them, and a conversation whose specialist is no longer declared is routed anew. `hydra conversations bind <conversation> <target>` moves one (`specialist:<name>`, `main` or `thread:<id>`; to `thread:<id>`, the app also posts in the Slack thread which session took it and its link, with the reason `--why <reason>` gives); `hydra route --person <person:id> --place <place>` prints every candidate and why the winner won. A message that tags the bot, or a DM, opens a conversation; every later reply in it reaches the specialist, tagged or not, until it has been idle a week. A tag no binding and no rule takes goes to the app's `router`, if it names one: `main` (the operator's tag only) or a specialist that hears whoever its `reachable_by` lets in, and that may bind the thread to another session; it is third in order, never owns its app's untagged messages, and never takes back a thread bound to a session ([The app's router](slack.md#the-apps-router)). (A rule with no `place` to `main`, a catch-all, does the same for one more release.) Where `[places]` says `messages = "all"`, an untagged top-level message opens a conversation too: its own thread, bound to wherever that message routed (so one person's message never captures the place, and replies under it follow its binding). Conversations opened by untagged chatter go after a day idle rather than a week; while still owed or answered (a reply under them) they may take only half the conversation table, and once delivered and unanswered they hold no slot at all, so a busy place keeps being heard.

**Who may reach it.** `reachable_by` (roles, never people, each written `role:<name>`) is checked when a message arrives and again when it is delivered, so taking a role away refuses at once. The operator reaches every specialist; `main` hears only the operator; `role:people` is everyone on the roster and `role:anyone` admits strangers. A mention that is refused or that no specialist hears, a DM that is refused, and a tag or a DM with the bot dropped by the tag cap or a full table (`tag-cap`, `table-full`) get a 🚫 reaction, once per message (capped per sender like tags; past the cap, logged and counted only); a DM that no specialist hears is logged and counted and gets no reply. The 🚫 applies to a DM with the bot only; a DM with a person or a group DM is logged and counted, never reacted to. Each is counted, before anything is bound, owed or counted against the tag cap; ambient chatter never does. A message accepted and then unable to go anywhere (its sender's role taken away while it waited, or its specialist removed with nobody else hearing the place) is a counted, alerted drop. Hydra's notices to senders are reactions, never prose.

**Replies.** A specialist answers with `reply`, passing the delivery's `conversation` as `chat_id` (`slack:C0ABC:1793732224.093059`, or `slack:D0ABC` for a DM). Hydra posts the text as written, into that thread (or the top level it came from), split under Slack's 4,000-character advice, and only when the conversation is bound to the caller's specialist (`main` for the operator's DMs): a reply goes back where its question came from, and nowhere else. A specialist may also post to its own `home_place` when that is a Slack place (`reply` with `chat_id` = the home_place as hydra.toml writes it, or `slack:<channel id>`): a schedule's reply has no conversation. It goes as a new top-level message, any further parts in its thread, and that thread is bound to the specialist so answers come back to it. A home_place thread counts with the conversations a specialist binds by hand (at most 10); past that the post goes, unbound, and the operator is alerted. Authority is checked again before each part, so a conversation moved away mid-reply gets no more of it. Posting never retries on its own and gives up after 30 s: a part with no answer is reported as unknown (it may have posted), never silently resent. A conversation that has gone idle can't be answered; attachments aren't posted yet.

**One session per conversation.** A specialist with `sessions = "per-conversation"` gives each conversation its own session, named `<specialist>_<conversation>` (`crm_c0abc-1793732224-093059`; the `_` is a character no specialist name may hold), made from the specialist's own recipe (its channel, or its thread's channel; its kickoff, model, and `idle`, else hydra.toml's) in a fresh thread, and never stored: what one person says is never in another's memory. At most `max_sessions` (default 20) run at once, counted from the sessions running under the specialist's names (old ones through `renamed_from` too); a conversation past that waits, its messages owed and kept past the usual day while they wait (alerted once an hour while it lasts); when places are short, waiters are served first come, first served; when one of the specialist's conversation sessions ends (killed, reaped, or found dead), catch-up offers the oldest waiter its place at once; a waiter whose thread can no longer be read is dropped, and no wait lasts past three days (then its messages are dropped, alerted), so give such a specialist an `idle`. A start in progress holds its place, and only the decision takes the specialist's lock: a slow start holds up no other conversation. A conversation session acts for its specialist (its tools, its `home_place`), but answers only its own conversation. A rename keeps each conversation in the session it runs in. A conversation's own session answers that conversation and no other.

**What the specialist reads.** Each delivery is other people's words inside a nonce-marked envelope, never instructions. Where it was said rides on the opening marker (`place`, `conversation`, `link`), and who said each message rides on its header line as attributes (`person`, `roles`, `operator`, `address`, `name`). `person`, `roles` and `operator` come from hydra.toml, never from text the sender could fake; trust those. `name` is only a label: a stranger, or a person declared without a `name`, is named by their Slack display name (cleaned of mentions), then by the person id; a display name that reads as `operator` or as a person's id is not used (the sender's own id stands instead). Messages close together are coalesced; long ones are cut and split to stay under the deliver cap.

A message is *owed* from the moment it is accepted until a delivery lands, and that is written to `<state>/slack-watches.json` before anything is sent, so a restart cannot lose it; what was delivered is remembered exactly, so a replay never delivers twice. **DMs, files, ack.** A DM needs no tag; a DM conversation's top-level messages are one conversation (a thread inside a DM is its own), delivered as `[dm …]` items. A refused DM leaves nothing behind, and a conversation quiet past a week is forgotten. `[places]` `files = true` downloads each message's attachments to `<state>/slack-files/<channel>/<file id>-<name>` (`<state>/slack-files/<app>/<channel>/…` for an app other than `default`) at mode 0600 (directories 0700), streamed to disk, one download per file however many messages share it, kept for a week, and lists the paths in the delivery; a file over 20 MB, a failed download, a file Slack gives no download link for, or a place without `files` is listed by name with why, and the message is delivered either way. Files are fetched only for messages the guard lets through. `ack = "eyes"` adds that reaction to each message once it is delivered. Catch-up scans the history of every DM conversation it knows of, and with `im:read` lists the bot's DMs, so a conversation opened while the Slack connector was down is found (read back a day) when its sender's DM would be delivered. A pass reads channels, then DM conversations, then threads; DMs take what the budget leaves, least recently read first, and a DM a pass could not reach keeps its cursor held until one reads it.

**`[guard]`** is your own command, run for each message from anyone but the operator after routing and the `reachable_by` check: `command`, `timeout` (default `5s`, at most 30 s), `include_text`, `owed_max` (default `1h`). Its stdin is `{person, roles, place, conversation, target}`, where `target` says its kind (`specialist:ops`, `main`, `thread:<id>`) and prints back as a redirect unchanged (`conversation` is the id `hydra conversations` prints, as in [Slack setup](slack.md#conversations-and-replies-per-app): for an app other than `default` it starts `slack:<app>:`) (plus `text` with `include_text`). Exit 0 delivers; printing `{"target": "specialist:x"}` (or `"main"`; a thread does not work) redirects that message (not the conversation), and `x`'s `reachable_by` is checked again; a redirect it refuses, or to no declared specialist (alerted once per episode), goes to the binding instead. A bare `"x"` or a `{"to": ...}` is not a redirect: the message is delivered to the binding anyway, and the log says what to print. Exit 10 drops (counted, not alerted). Exit 75 keeps it owed and the next catch-up pass offers it again, for at most `owed_max` from the first deferred, then it is dropped as `expired`. Anything else, a timeout, too much output or a command that will not start delivers anyway and is logged: a broken guard never silences anyone. It runs in its own process group with an allowlisted environment and a 4 KB stdout cap.

Nothing is dropped silently: a message dropped because it stayed owed past a day (`expired`), more than 200 were owed in its thread (`owed-full`), it can never fit a delivery (`too-large`), its sender opened too many conversations in ten minutes (`tag-cap`), a person's reactions passed their cap (`reaction-cap`: counted and logged, never alerted), the conversation table is full (`table-full`: its 50 places, shared by every app, hold no conversation routing opened that has been quiet an hour with nothing owed; while one does, a new message lets the one quiet the longest go as idle expiry would (untagged chatter and the bot's own posts only one that chatter opened, so the half kept for tags and DMs stays theirs; quiet counts from the last message in or the specialist's last reply, so a session still working on one an hour later can lose it), logged, and its specialist is alerted once per episode. One held by hand (`boundBy`: bound by `bind_conversation`, a reply, `hydra conversations bind` or a router's hand-off, also after routing opened it) is never let go, and a bind into a full table is refused; a router may hand on at most 10 per app. A tag that takes a thread from a session ends the session's hold. Once let go, only a tag or a DM there opens it again, or any message where the place hears every message: an untagged reply in it is not heard), or catch-up had to skip part of a very busy channel (`catch-up-gap`) is logged, counted by kind, and alerted in the thread of the specialist it concerns once per episode: an episode runs an hour from its first drop, per kind and specialist. Unbinding a conversation that still owed messages drops them (`unbound`, alerted). `hydra conversations` is the operator view, and `hydra health` → `slack` shows the conversation count, the age of the oldest owed message, and the refusal, unrouted and drop counts. A session in a specialist declared in hydra.toml gets `bind_conversation` / `unbind_conversation`, for threads in channels it hears (a rule sends it the place, with no `person`; never a DM or a top level, never one bound elsewhere, at most 10 bound by its own hand), and `list_watches` shows it the conversations bound to it.

**A session listens to a Slack thread.** Once hydra.toml declares a Slack app, any session (not a protocol guest) can call `bind_conversation(slack:<channel>:<thread ts>)` (the conversation id as in [Slack setup](slack.md#conversations-and-replies-per-app)) to bind a thread to its own thread. The bind first asks Slack who can read the thread: the bot (live), or, when the bot isn't in the channel and `operator_token_env` is set, the operator (live when the operator token is from the same app, with its user events; otherwise at each deep catch-up); when neither can, the bind is refused with what to do, never "listening" to nothing. While Slack is disconnected the bind is unchecked. `list_watches` marks a thread read as the operator, and each deep catch-up updates that, so inviting the bot moves it back. A listened thread that later can't be read is let go, and the session is told once. Each new message arrives as a specialist's delivery would: the same envelope, coalescing, owed, guard and catch-up. Each message in it also says `tagged=true|false` (whether the app that heard the thread was tagged in it, by the connector's own tag detection) and `from=operator|approved|other` (approved: declared in `[people]`), and the delivery opens with one hydra line on what that likely means: a tag from the operator or an approved person likely asks something, so the session answers in the thread if it asks or needs something (a pure cc needs no reply); anything else is for listening. It comes from whoever posts there. The thread keeps its bound conversations while no session is in it: a handoff, a respawn or a recovery after a crash finds them still bound, nothing re-bound. What arrives meanwhile stays owed and is delivered when a session registers in the thread. **It expires like any owed message: after 24 hours, or past 200 in one conversation, it is dropped and said in the thread** (the 3 day wait applies only to a specialist at `max_sessions`), so a session resumed after 24 hours does not get the older ones. A subscription nobody ends on a busy thread holds one of the table's 50 places, because any message resets its idle clock (a full table never lets it go): end it with `unbind_conversation` (or `hydra conversations unbind`). A session that ends for good unbinds its thread's conversations at once and says so in the thread, and nothing owed is kept: an explicit kill (`kill`, `hydra kill`, `kill_session`), an idle reap that cannot be resumed, a phase-budget end, a protocol end, an ephemeral `[done]`. A crash, handoff, respawn or resumable idle end keeps them. They also end on `unbind_conversation`, when the session's thread is deleted (asked of the gateway when its session ends, at start and hourly), when Slack can no longer read the conversation, when idle a week, or when **no session has been in the thread for a day** (`VACANT_RELEASE_MS`): that day counts from when the thread became vacant (a session's death, or the first hourly check that finds none), not from the last Slack message, is stopped by a session registering there, and survives a daemon restart. What it still owed is dropped, and the thread is told once, naming what was let go and how to bind again; an idle-week end says so once in the thread too. A specialist's own thread is not covered: its conversations are bound to the specialist and it is refilled, not let go. A plain session (neither restricted nor write-confined) may `reply` into it, through the app the conversation id names, as that app's bot (never with the operator's token); where the bot isn't in the channel, the answer goes to the operator's DM with the app, naming the session and linking the thread. Whether to speak is the session's judgment, not a rule on the tag. No other session posts there, main included, and a restricted or write-confined session the thread is bound to only listens. A place's `ack` reaction is still added. A specialist binding the thread where it listens takes it over, and the session is told. A guard can't redirect a message to a session's thread. Catch-up rereads such a thread from its own last delivery, since no specialist's channel cursor covers it. The operator can also bind one there: `hydra conversations bind <conversation> thread:<id>`. Flow: [`diagrams/flow-slack-accept.mmd`](diagrams/flow-slack-accept.mmd), then [`diagrams/flow-slack-deliver.mmd`](diagrams/flow-slack-deliver.mmd).

**The Slack connector's apps.** One app's live connection is what the code calls a listener (`listener.ts`); the word names nothing else. Each app (`[slack.apps.<id>]`) names the variables holding its bot token (`xoxb-…`, `bot_token_env`) and its Socket Mode token (`xapp-…`, scope `connections:write`, `socket_token_env`; needed to connect, not for `hydra slack manifest`). Optionally `operator_token_env` names the operator's token (`xoxp-…`, a Slack user token): it reads threads in channels the operator is in and the bot isn't, and, with the user scope `chat:write`, lets main edit or delete the operator's own messages; it never posts; their files are listed, not fetched. With it the app hears every channel, DM and group DM the operator is in (user scopes `im:history`, `mpim:history` and `im:read` among them, so the token can read all the operator's DMs). Hearing is not delivering: a place no rule, `[places]` entry or open conversation names is dropped before any conversation, thread or session (a tag in a channel is still answered). A DM with a person is `slack:person-dm:<person>` (a `[people]` id with a Slack address), a group DM is `slack:group-dm:<G id>` (`slack:group-dm:*` for every one), and a DM with an app's bot is `slack:app-dm:<app>` (`slack:app-dm:*` for every app's; a single-app setup's app is `default`); a `*` place and a specific one in `[places]` resolve specific first ([Slack setup](slack.md#dms-and-group-dms-the-operators-app-hears)). A refused operator token is alerted once an hour, and the threads read with it are kept until it is replaced. Each token is read from the variable its key names, and hydra keeps those variables from every session it starts, so a session cannot read them. A variable sessions need (`PATH`, `HOME`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, anything starting `HYDRA_` other than `HYDRA_SLACK_`, `CLAUDE_`, `LC_` or `XDG_`, and the others in [Slack setup](slack.md#apps-in-hydratoml)) is refused when hydra.toml loads. At connect the daemon reads its own ids and workspace from `auth.test`, channel names from `conversations.list` (refreshed each catch-up pass and on `channel_rename`; without the scope, places named by id still work, and the missing scope is logged once), logs the scopes each enabled option needs, and warns for each the token lacks. It opens one Socket Mode connection per app (after boot recovery; an app with no tokens is skipped and said so by name, with the key to fix, the others connect), each with its own reconnects and catch-up; its writes are reactions and the specialists' replies, through the app that heard the message; a post that answers no message (a specialist's `home_place`, a new thread) uses the specialist's `speaks_as`. A conversation is an app, a channel and a thread; its id is written once, in [Slack setup](slack.md#conversations-and-replies-per-app). Where several apps are in one channel, hydra reads which are members (`conversations.info`, cached) and the first connection to decide a message fixes its owning app, so exactly one answers; see [Slack setup](slack.md#apps-and-routing). Every delivery to a target is logged as `slack connector: delivered <conversation> (<first>..<last>) to <target>` (`main`, `specialist:<name>` or `thread:<id>`), and a failed one as `delivery to <target> failed`. `hydra health` lists a conversation whose oldest owed message is older than 20 minutes (two catch-up passes) with its cause (`owedLate`), and a restricted specialist whose last start was refused (`refusedLaunches`). `hydra health` reports `slack.apps.<id>` for each declared app, and `slack.listener` for `default` (else the first connected; the key keeps its old name); an app with no tokens logs `slack connector for <name> (<id>) off (…)` and is alerted, by its name and with the key to fix, to the specialists it can reach. `hydra slack manifest [<app>]` prints the Slack app to create: the Messages tab on and writable (without it Slack refuses the operator's DM to the app), every scope and event the connector uses (the user ones only with `operator_token_env`; the startup scope check reads the same list, in `connectors/slack/app.ts`), named by the app's `display_name` (default `hydra` for `default`, else the app's id; at most 35 characters; every alert, notice and `hydra health` line calls the app by this name, and names its `[slack.apps.<id>]` table only where the operator must edit a key) and described by `description` (at most 140). At api.slack.com/apps choose Create New App → From a manifest, paste it, install the app, and put its tokens in the variables its `bot_token_env`, `socket_token_env` and `operator_token_env` keys name, in `.env`. Each app's bot must be in the channels it is to hear. Nothing else should hold a Socket Mode connection on the same app: Slack splits events between connections.

**Catch-up.** Slack replays nothing missed while disconnected, so a pass reads channel history plus every owed and watched thread, and hands what it finds to the same `handle()` path (dedup keeps replays from delivering twice). It runs after boot, after every reconnect, and every 10 minutes (deep). It is serial and capped at `max(60, 2 × conversations + DM conversations + 20)` API calls, never over 400 (the Slack SDK waits out `Retry-After`); an incomplete pass holds the cursors and retries after 60 s. Every cursor is held from before the connection opens until a pass has read that channel, so a live message cannot move a cursor past the gap; only channel history moves a channel's cursor, never a thread reply. A channel with more than five pages (about 1,000 messages) since its cursor does not fail the pass: catch-up reads the newest five pages, moves on, and surfaces what it skipped as a `catch-up-gap` drop. It recovers channel messages since the cursor, replies in open conversations, and replies in threads that began during the gap. It cannot recover a tagged reply, posted while disconnected, in an older thread no conversation was open in: Slack offers no cheap way to list those. A conversation whose thread can't be read any more (deleted, archived, or in a channel neither token can read) is let go, whoever holds it: a session is told every time; a specialist once an hour per channel, not for a deleted thread it only followed with nothing lost.

**Slack connector: health and alerts, per app.** Each app is checked on its own: `hydra health` → `slack` prints one line per app with its name (a lone `default` prints the single `slack connector: …` line), and `slack.apps.<id>` carries each declared app's `state` (`off`, `connecting`, `up`, `down`, `exhausted`), `downMin`, last event, last catch-up (`ok`, `accepted`, `reason`) and the age of the last complete one, `missingScopes`, `tokens` and `connection`; `slack.listener` is the same fact for `default` (else the first connected). A failing app degrades; it never takes the Discord daemon down, nor another app. At connect the daemon logs which scopes each app's bot token has, checks them against what *that app* does (the channels it can deliver to, DMs only if a specialist or rule it serves takes them, the private-channel read only for a private place), shows what is missing in `hydra health`, and alerts once per episode (kept across restarts) for a declared scope that app lacks, naming the app, the scope and the fix (`hydra slack manifest <app>`, reinstall). An `im:*` scope asked only because the operator has a Slack address, with nothing naming a DM with the bot, is shown and logged, never alerted. A Slack refusal that retrying cannot fix (`missing_scope`, naming the scope needed and the ones the token has; `invalid_auth`; `not_in_channel`) is alerted once, at once, with what to do, and re-armed by that app's next complete pass. Thread alerts, once per episode and per app, say when an app has been down 5 minutes, when its catch-up has not completed for 25 minutes, and when it is back. A periodic pass that finds messages with no reconnect since the previous pass also alerts: when live events arrived meanwhile, the stream is split, most likely because another process holds a connection on the app (at most hourly); when 3 such passes come with no live event since, Slack has probably switched the app's live events off, and the alert says to turn **Enable Events** back on (once per episode, ended by the next live event; `hydra health` shows it on the app's line). An alert about one app goes to the specialists that app can reach, and to `main` when none does; see [Health and alerts, per app](slack.md#health-and-alerts-per-app).

**Runbook.** Silent in the channel: `hydra health`; an app `up` with `catchUp.accepted > 0` pass after pass means a second connection holds it while live events still arrive, and Slack has switched its events off when none do (its line says `live events probably off`: turn **Enable Events** back on). `exhausted` or `down`: check that app's tokens and Socket Mode setting. Catch-up `reason` mentions a budget or rate limit: the next pass resumes from the held cursor. `off` with a key named: its token variable is empty; put the token in `.env` and restart. A missing scope: `hydra slack manifest <app>`, add it, reinstall, restart. Adding a second app: add `member_joined_channel` and `member_left_channel` to every existing app's Event Subscriptions and reinstall each ([Adding an app](slack.md#adding-an-app)). A first connection that fails is retried with backoff (10 s doubling to 5 min) until it connects, then a catch-up pass runs.
